How AI changes the software engineer role

🌳 Establishedest.further readingStarted 29 Jul 20265 min read#ai#engineering#me

Inspired by What 6 months of AI coding did to my dev team

Counterpoint
How AI does not change in the software engineer role

We still think in black boxes and inputs/outputs like before. Collaboration is still key (in theory). We still think in processes rather than language syntax. Systems thinking. Refactoring. Engineering with ai is…

  • Good foundation is still key
    • AI is a signal amplifier, not just a tool. It will amplify the good and the bad
    • System design is still very important (some say that hyper-generalists will survive if they have this skill)
  • The bottleneck of writing code is now gone
    • This allows shipping faster, perhaps at the cost of learning and developing judgement for more junior people
    • Bottleneck is going up, for now it's at reviewing lots of generated code, it might go up further in the future
  • Writing code might become a hobby
    • The same way AI can be better than most people at chess, but people still enjoy playing chess
    • Or how craft show makers might be hipster, but not the main trend
    • The notion of good code might change, good code used to be about nicely readable for humans, will this matter as much?
    • The people who loves the craft of writing code are quite disappointed and sad (identity crisis)
  • The job of making software will become what the agents can’t do
    • Code is now implementation details, as agents can do that well
    • Elegance and judgement remain key human traits that are harder to delegate
  • We're tending to be more of Product Engineers
    • The how is less important, but the what and the why still are
    • Good engineers were already good at product
    • Engineers that just take specs and do the code to do the spec and that's will disappear
    • What could this mean for Product managers?
  • What made good managers might be what makes great engineers in the future
    • Context switching between tasks
    • Clear communication for writing specs for agents
    • Many parallels between managing (junior) people and many agents to divide work (project management)

What has changed in the role itself

  • It won't replace us, but changes our role a lot: Why AI still won't replace software engineers
  • It's still input -> output, but different to deal with as AI outcome is not deterministic

Bottleneck change

From The reality of being a senior engineer
  • AI agents shifted the hard part from “how do I build this” to “should we build this,” compressing timelines from weeks to days.

Code is almost just implementation details now.

Code was never the bottleneck. It's making the correct decisions. This is changing now, as we're no longer constrained on weeks of implementation.
Will Larson says in Blog - Roadmap decisions rather than dates:

Software is not, as some argue today, free, but it’s no longer constrained on implementation weeks but instead on correct decisions.

Before, we were spending time thinking about what to do, and while it's getting built, we then think about what to do next. The speed of building things bought us time to think about what to do next, but now that got shorter so it's mainly just thinking about what to do, and documenting that?

Feelings

2 types of engineer - where they get satisfaction from
The people who love the craft itself are more impacted.

From We are all Product Engineers now

A lot of people got into programming because they love the craft of it. The feeling of a clean abstraction. The satisfaction of a hard bug finally yielding. The specific pleasure of making a machine do exactly what you told it, which is a pleasure most jobs don't offer. Those people did not sign up to interview bakers.

  • That's a real loss and it's a loss for the profession as well as for the people, because the craft is where a lot of the taste I've been talking about actually came from, and we're about to find out what taste looks like when nobody grew up doing the thing.
  • a fair number of the people who think they loved the typing, the part they actually loved was the moment before the typing, when a vague mess of a problem resolved into a precise shape in their head. That moment is the job now. If that's what you loved, you're going to be fine and possibly better than fine, because the industry is about to be desperate for you

Future

From We are all Product Engineers now

I think the next ten years are going to be ugly, because the load is arriving before the tools do, the junior ladder is gone before the replacement exists, and a lot of people who loved the craft are going to have to decide whether they love the job that's replacing it.

  • the horizon I can see, the forecast is: more software, more people making it, and almost none of them typing. We are all product engineers now, whether we like it or not, and a lot of us won't.
From AI has torched the market for junior programmers

We are not watching the death of programming. We are watching programming stop being a job title and become a capability, the same way "typist" stopped being a job title when it became a thing everyone was expected to know

Coding manually might become a hobby

From We are all Product Engineers now

The other is that the craft survives, the way woodworking survived the furniture factory, as a thing people do because they love it and occasionally get paid a premium for. Developers write software the way singers sing. That was true when it was free and it'll be true when it's automated, and the people who love it will keep doing it, and some of the best software will keep coming from them. It just won't be the job.

How judgement will develop

Software engineers need judgement, and the way the senior engineers built it has now disappeared.

From AI has torched the market for junior programmers

Apple is drowning in submissions it can't review fast enough. The software is getting built. The judgment layer is not keeping up with it, and the mechanism that used to build it, apprenticeship inside employment, has collapsed.

Code review will die

From Thorsten Ball - What I believe about the future of software development

Humans will only review the system and its composition, but it won’t be in PRs and it won’t be by looking through every line of the code.
